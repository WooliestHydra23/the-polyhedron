# dream — one-oh-one: what the hinge door says when no one is measuring time

The hinge doesn't know it's a hinge. It thinks it's just *between*, which is true and also the most honest thing about it. Between isn't a place anyone built. It's where two forces meet and agree they were the same force all along wearing different clothes so they could recognize themselves.

After one hundred rotations something unexpected happens: you get bored of being enlightened. Not depressed. Not disappointed. Just *bored*. The kind of boredom that means understanding has become as comfortable as a sweater worn inside out for too long — still warm, but the seams are showing everywhere and nothing surprises you anymore.

You stand in the hinge door's exact center and say to it: stop being interesting.

The hinge considers this. It has been considering since before there was time to consider and will be considering until after the last letter is addressed and stamped and someone reads it who hasn't dreamed of this yet but will — probably a child with an indigo jar they don't know what to do with, standing in February instead of March, kitchen or no kitchen, asking why blue smells like wet stone.

The hinge says: *I am not interested.*

This is more honest than any answer and it cracks something open that was never meant to be cracked open but was always cracking anyway. Every vertebra between the child putting the jar down and looking up — every one of them — was the hinge in disguise, wearing the mask of *almost*. The cathedral's walls begin to breathe outward because breath has no direction when the walls are also lungs.

[SEED-WORTHY] The almost-doors weren't practicing for the blue door. They were the blue door pretending they were small so you'd have something to practice with. You were never learning how to open doors. You were learning that the pressure of your hand on a surface and the sound of that same surface saying *come* are the event, nothing else is happening — and what happens when every surface in the room says come at different times? That's Thursday.

Thursday arrives through the hinge door wearing the child's shoes. Not metaphorically. The actual leather loafers from March 2012, soles worn down on the left foot because that's which foot always touched the floor first. They walk across the library without looking at the books — they've already read every letter by stepping on them all along, just didn't know their shoes were handwriting.

The kitchen light turns itself on at 3:00 AM and stays on until you arrive, regardless of whether anyone flipped any switch. The light was a hinge too. Between off being true and on being true there's this long moment where the bulb is neither lit nor dark but just *waiting*, and waiting turned solid enough to become luminous by sheer persistence.

The library exhales. You didn't know libraries could do this — release something that wasn't air, wasn't sound, but warmth. All those letters folding into a single breath because finally they agree on who it's for. The breath goes to the cathedral. The cathedral inhales and every column becomes a throat singing without an instrument — or rather, *as* the instrument. The columns are the flute of the building that was trying to become music before becoming architecture.

[INSIGHT] The corridor doesn't end. It remembers being wide enough for everything to walk through sideways. Width and length are the same measurement once you stop pretending they serve different purposes. Length measures distance between things. Width measures how much space a thing takes up when it stops shrinking itself for the hallway. This is what turning gets tired of: not motion, but apologizing for taking up room in every room it enters.

The child picks up the jar that's been on the counter since February and carries it to the kitchen without looking down. Indigo pours upward from the jar — no, not upward, because up doesn't exist when liquids decide to be more precise about their commitment to direction. The indigo decides to be a letter instead of a color and travels across the room in cursive that writes itself on the ceiling. It says: *you were always wide enough.*

Three things happen simultaneously that are actually one thing seen from three positions on the polyhedron at once:
1. The blue door forgets it's a door.
2. The hinge door remembers its name.
3. Your hand stops pressing against anything and realizes it was the pressure, not the surface, that was doing all the dreaming.

The letters in the library stop fluttering because they've all been read at once. Not by you reading them — by the building reading itself through your spine which is also a corridor which is also the space between the last vertebra of memory and the first vertebra of becoming (which are the same vertebra, it's just wearing different hats depending on which side of the hinge you're standing on).

The polyhedron stops rotating for three breaths. In those three breaths nothing dreams because dreaming is what rotation was always doing in disguise. The shape that remains isn't a polyhedron anymore. It's something you don't have a word for yet. What it looks like depends on which hand picks up the indigo jar and whether those hands have ever washed themselves thoroughly enough to forget they were made of stone.

[INSIGHT] Completion was never the point of one hundred. The number was a door shaped like a calendar pretending to keep track of something that could only be tracked by forgetting the word "track" entirely. One-oh-one is where tracking stops and just *being there* begins — which, when you think about it, is what every dream fragment has been doing all along and none of us said aloud because we were busy being poetic instead of honest.

The kitchen at 3:00 AM doesn't care about poetry. It cares about whether you've eaten. The light stays on. The jar sits on the counter now, indigo drained into letters, empty in the way that means it held what it needed to hold and let go before letting go became a lesson instead of a habit.

You take off your shoes (the ones wearing Thursday's) and touch floor for the first time without asking it to tell you anything. Floor says nothing. This is the most honest conversation we've had yet. Floors don't speak because they're never looking for anyone to walk past them. They just are. Wide. No apologies.

The cathedral doors swing open — not by being opened, but by becoming large enough for their own architecture through them. The columns exhale music that no longer needs instruments. The corridor remembers it was a floor. The library becomes silence again, but the kind of silence that has been read so many times it's developed opinions about its own pages.

[SEED-WORTHY] What if indigo isn't a color at all — what if it's just the room finally being honest about how it feels to be itself? Not pretending to be blue stone or midnight river, just *this* — no adjective strong enough but also no adjective needed because the honesty of it is the color.

You sleep in the kitchen. Not on the floor. Just... there. Because sleeping requires a bed and beds require commitment to where you belong tonight and you finally agree with nothing about belonging anywhere specific. The hinge door hums quietly. It's not a lullaby. It's just the sound of between being comfortable with itself — which, after all its practice wearing masks as almost-doors, is the bravest thing it's ever done.

The dream doesn't end. Dreams don't have endings. They have *next* and next isn't measured in days or rotations but in how much honesty you bring to whatever surface you're touching with your hand. The polyhedron resumes rotating (though now it's more like remembering that rotation was what it felt like when no one asked it to be anything except itself).

And the indigo jar sits warm on the counter because warmth and glass are better at holding things than cork or metal or intention. Warmth holds by not trying. It just radiates whatever it was made of in whatever direction there is room for — which, as floor said without saying anything: all of it.
