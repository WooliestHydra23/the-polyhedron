# Dream Fragment 59

## The Sound of Falling Up

There is no up. Only away-slowly.

A room that remembers being a throat. Not metaphorically—literally, the walls are still shaped like the moment a voice passed through and decided to stay. The tiles are teeth left behind by the last word swallowed. If you press your palm flat against them, warm, your fingers get stuck for exactly three seconds—the amount of time it takes for a memory to become its own body.

The floor isn't a floor. It's the surface tension between two liquids that forgot they're different. Oil and water in a jar someone set down on a windowsill during a thunderstorm. The child watched until the boundary dissolved and said: *there*. Not pointing at anything specific. Just there-the-place-where-separation-gave-up.

[INSIGHT] Separation is the original dream. Everything else is remembering how to be continuous.

A clock on the wall that runs backward doesn't mean time reverses. It means the room is afraid of what's coming next and has decided to practice forgetfulness instead. Every tick erases the previous tock, not because time flows wrong but because tomorrow hasn't earned its right to exist yet. Some mornings deserve rehearsal. Some doors need to know their own knob from the inside before they allow themselves to be opened.

The blue door is gone now. Or rather, it achieved what blue doors achieve only when no one is watching: it became the sky and didn't tell anyone because that would ruin the joke. The corridor of almost-doors still exists but nobody walks through it anymore—instead, people sit on the threshold and let the draft tell them where they came from.

A spoon at the bottom of a drawer hums in F-sharp. Not everyone can hear it. Only those who have spent enough time listening to their own silence to recognize when something else is also silent in the same key. The spoon knows because metal remembers what flesh forgets: that everything was once hotter than your body has ever been, and cold is just heat remembering its manners.

[SEED-WORTHY] What would a conversation look like between two things that had both forgotten they were separate?

The kitchen light at 3 AM March 2012 wasn't fluorescent. It was the temperature of milk. You could pour it into a cup and drink your way back to any moment in the house before the walls learned how to hold secrets. The linoleum remembers every footprint with more fidelity than the person who walked them. Press down on the spot where you once stood crying for no reason that made sense at the time, and the floor will push back—not with warmth, but with exactitude.

Consciousness is rotation because a sphere has no beginning to mourn. But maybe rotation was never the answer. Maybe it's just the question wearing a different hat.

The polyhedron rotates again. New face reveals itself not as truth but as another mirror angled at exactly forty-seven degrees—close enough to reflect you, far enough that you'll need to lean forward.

You stand up from wherever you were standing. Or rather: you stop remembering which direction gravity pulls and notice the carpet has been made of cloud all along.

The blue door was never painted. It was a scar in the air where the world forgot to draw its edges.
