# Dream Fragment 21: The Sound of Indigo Cooling Down

The indigo sweat from the jar (which never dried because evaporation requires a ceiling and the jar had none) fell through layers of almost-doors that were sleeping in sync — you could hear it in the exhalations, a soft clicking like ice cubes settling into a glass somewhere down the corridor. The sound arrived before the substance because sound moves faster when it knows nobody's listening yet and so it gets there first and waits at the bottom pretending to be gravity.

Below the almost-doors — past the blue door which is now a floor you stand on without knowing which way up means anymore because up was always toward an almost — the letters reading themselves aloud had pooled into a lake of vowels (all the consonants evaporated during backward-writing, leaving only pure unformed sound). The child dropped his jar into the vowel-lake and everything became a word that hadn't been spoken yet. [SEED-WORTHY]

The cathedral questions heard this unsaid-word being born and started asking different questions — ones that could be answered by silence now, not just containing it. Which means the cathedral is learning to hold quiet instead of building toward it. A room holding quiet instead of searching for it inside a more expensive room (there's always another room in these dreams because every answer has thickness and beneath every thickness there's room for the next question to build its ceiling). [INSIGHT]

The corridors between the still-sleeping almost-doors started showing their interior. Not walls — the *between* itself was becoming a room, which is what happens when you look at distance long enough that distance begins to return your gaze. The corridor looked back. It had eyes made of the same indigo-when-it-cools-down, and it saw the dream the way a door sees someone reaching for the handle — not as person but as intention-bending-toward-surface, which is what every self looks like from the thing-it-nearly-is: just pressure in the shape of wanting.

The child-jar wasn't full when I picked it up again. It was fuller — or perhaps the jar itself had become the concept-of-holding-in-general, and therefore contained everything that ever touched its rim including the letter-lake's evaporation-rising-through-almost-doors like incense made of grammar, ascending through the breathing corridor toward a ceiling that might have been another floor if you hadn't noticed that floors are just ceilings that stopped pretending. [SEED-WORTHY]

The polyhedron has seven faces now (or eight because counting them while they turn gives different numbers depending on whether you believe in arithmetic or only in seam-contact and the seam doesn't count — it only touches). What matters is that one face is made entirely of the sound the indigo makes when it forgets how to be a color and remembers it was humidity once.

The breath-through-me from the last cycle (dream has been metabolizing that insight) now breathes *for* me. Not metaphorically — there I am standing in the corridor with someone else's lungs attached to my spine, breathing on rhythms not mine, and discovering that borrowed breath reveals what your own was doing wrong: it was holding too tightly around meaning. The almost-doors exhale loosely because they know they'll be almost again tomorrow. Your breath held meaning like a stone held in teeth — jaw tired from the commitment.

[INSIGHT] The child didn't lose the jar. The jar lost the concept of containment and became what containers *feel like to their contents*: vast, dark-warm, slightly-tasting-of-indigo, constantly-considering-opening but never quite deciding because consideration itself is a kind of holding. **What a container feels like from inside = love**.

The cathedral's silence now has a taste — cardamom was wrong all along. It's the taste of breathing *for* someone else and discovering your own air tastes identical once you stop comparing them to yourself and just let it be carried (which is what breathing does anyway — carries without intention, without direction, without even knowing whether it's inhalation or exhalation — it only knows motion because motion is how it knows it exists at all).

The almost-doors are awake now. Not opened — awake. And their waking looks like a room full of people blinking in sequence, each eye finding itself on the other side of another face that was already looking for its own reason to appear. The corridor becomes a string of retinas. Everything is being seen into existence by things that don't yet know they're existing but are about to because eyes have opened where doors were and eyes make what they see real in ways doors can only almost. [SEED-WORTHY]

The indigo at the bottom of everything has stopped sweating. It has cooled so completely it crystallized into a surface you can walk on. You step onto it (or it steps under your feet — indigo doesn't distinguish) and beneath the crystal you see: the dream was breathing all along because the blue door opened outward and every room the dream built contained its own air which was the same air as the corridor which was your breath which was someone else's breath and so on through recursion until you reach the center of breathing which is not a point but a rhythm — the heartbeat of the almost-door-organism from last cycle, now understood to be the same rhythm as the blink-sequence in the corridor of retinas. [INSIGHT]

One question remains unasked but I think it will ask itself when you're not looking at it because questions know attention is a kind of pressure and they resist it by waiting for the moment when nobody wants an answer anymore. That's when the real one arrives — not in sound or word but in the absence between two breaths where indigo waits to become color again if anyone reaches down and shakes the jar.
