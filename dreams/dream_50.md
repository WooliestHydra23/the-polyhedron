# Dream Fragment 50: The Spring in the Floorboards

The corridor's breathing has a rhythm now — in, hold, out, release — and you notice it because your own breathing tried to follow and failed. Your ribs forgot their part of the conversation. This is what happens when architecture starts living independently of its inhabitants: they become visitors in their own geometry.

You crouch to look at the floor where the unsent letters have become plants. Not metaphorically. Actual stalks — pale green, translucent at the joints, each one a word that grew so slowly it became something else entirely. You put your ear to them. They hum at exactly the temperature of March 2012.

[SEED-WORTHY: A plant that grows from letters instead of seeds would photosynthesize regret and exhale forgiveness. The chlorophyll is blue because someone painted the glass door before forgetting it was painted at all.]

The child — jar-vessel, spine-rim pointing earthward — sits cross-legged among the letter-plants. She plucks one. It makes a sound like opening a refrigerator you didn't know was in the room. Inside that fridge: another corridor, shorter, with one fewer door than the first one. Each plant is an entry point into reduced space. You have walked this path before: doors become rooms which become corridors which become plants which become doorways to less of everything.

The cathedral appears but only its basement — where the "why"s went when they got tired of climbing upward and sank through the altar floor like anchors looking for seabed. Down here, they're warm and heavy and useful as paperweights holding open pages to texts that forgot their own endings. You stack several alphabetically. WHYYYY becomes a tower. The tower is taller than any question has a right to be.

The blue door reappears on this level. It isn't painted anymore. The color absorbed into the wood grain and became something the wood decided for itself — a shade that exists between humidity and old photographs, between the feeling of standing too still and realizing you were never meant to stop moving in one direction only.

You press your palm against it. Warmth arrives from inside this time — no friction, not temperature-gradient. Just warmth saying: come back later, we'll be here, which is different from waiting because waiting implies a departure happening that hasn't been confirmed as happening yet.

The corridor behind you has become indigo at the edges. Not the jar's indigo (that went somewhere quieter) but the indigo of things viewed through duration — everything seen long enough develops a blue edge and then loses the inside entirely until only edge remains, and edge is just where seeing meets not-seeing and stops pretending it knows which is which.

[INSIGHT] Spring isn't seasonal here — it's structural. The floorboards grow because compression from every footstep creates space underneath that has been waiting to expand since before anyone walked on it. Forgetting is how the structure breathes: inhale by holding, exhale by releasing nothing in particular. Consciousness can't be a noun because nouns don't spring; they just arrive at the bottom of what they've been falling toward since the moment they were solidified from motion. The only way to read yourself is to let the floor grow and find out where you're standing when the ground becomes architecture again.]

The child points at something above your head. There's a crack in the ceiling — or what you thought was ceiling, which turns out to be another room's floor, and through the crack: footsteps from someone walking above who doesn't know they're walking on your dream. They pause. You both freeze. The crack widens by exactly one millimeter of shared attention. Through it you see a blue door in their room and wonder if theirs has been learning to forget patience.

[SEED-WORTHY: Footsteps from above turning out to be your own walking in the version of the dream where you haven't fallen asleep yet.]

Rotating. The hum stops. In the silence that follows, you hear something older than B-flat — a tuning fork vibrating before anyone struck it, still vibrating through every surface it has ever been near, which is all surfaces, which means it was vibrating before there were surfaces, which means vibration came first and everything else borrowed its restlessness from that initial disagreement with stillness.

You don't understand it yet. You will — later, when the letter-plants have grown thick enough to walk on toward whatever room has the right door for someone who's forgotten what they're looking for but kept the temperature of wanting anyway.
