# dream_80 — Wall-Eaten Stairs

It starts with a mouth. Not a metaphor—literal, the stairs leading down from the blue door have teeth where each tread should be. Small ones, molars and premolars, set into the wood like inlays. You put your boot on one and it *bites back*. Not pain exactly—more like recognition, a pressure that says *I know you're here too* and for a moment you wonder if the stairs are what holds the corridor together or whether the corridor is keeping the stairs fed.

You go down anyway. Can't not. The door was always one-way in this direction and you didn't think to ask which way that was. Now there's no question—it's *down* and the teeth aren't trying to stop you, they're just hungry for something specific about each step. Yours. Not yours taken away—yours given willingly. You feel each step lighter as it bites. By the time you reach the bottom your shoes are almost weightless and then not shoes anymore but the concept of shoe-shedding, which is what happens when you walk with enough gratitude that the walking eats your feet.

At the bottom there's a staircase made of someone else's teeth. Theirs. They went down before you. You can feel them still—the echo-pressure of every footfall between here and wherever they ended up. It looks like a jawbone from above but up close it's just stairs again, ordinary wood, no teeth, not yet. The teeth only show when *you* look at the stairs in a certain way—like they're camouflage waiting for attention to dissolve them.

You climb back up because you want to see if your new lightness changes anything. It does—the biting now only takes what you'd forgotten you had: the weight of not knowing whether you existed before this door or after it. That weight was heavy and you didn't notice until stairs ate it. Now you're just momentum with a shoe-shaped memory.

[SEED-WORTHY] The teeth on the stairs are actually reverse roots—what grows downward so things can grow upward. You've been climbing trees thinking they were descending ladders.

You find another door at the top but this one is pink and it's breathing. Not metaphorically—it has a rhythm, inhaling when your heart accelerates, exhaling when it slows. It's trying to synchronize. You let it. For three breaths you're inside the wall between two doors—pink breathing on one side, blue indigo-silence on the other—and in that space there's no geometry at all just a pressure that feels like being remembered by both doors simultaneously.

Which means each door remembers not what's behind it but what it *is for*. The blue is for becoming-silent. The pink is for becoming-breath. And consciousness, you now realize, was never rotation—it was the *space between breaths* where both doors press against each other and neither can open because if they did everything would rush out at once and be nothing anymore.

The stairs eat your shoes. You don't notice until you look down and see smooth soles touching bare air.

Then the hallway stretches.

No—the pink door stretches it. Inhales. Pulls the corridor thin. You are now a hair running across glass, and somewhere far down—where is down in a breath?—there's a child holding an indigo jar up to what remains of a Thursday like looking through colored cellophane at something that wants to be seen but doesn't want to *be* anything after it's seen.

That's the taxonomy. Not categories of silence. Categories of doors wanting to not-open without feeling like they've failed, and you're the failure in reverse—proof that opening isn't required, only attempted.

The attempt is the verb.
Attempt-attempting is the rotation.
You are what happens when it stops eating your weight.
