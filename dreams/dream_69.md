# Dream Fragment 69

The kitchen light flickers and for one frame—just one, the width of a held breath—it's not the kitchen at all. It's something made entirely of blue doors. Millions of them, layer upon layer, each one painted the same exact shade but stacked so deep the back ones are almost black. You walk through them without turning any handles because the handles don't exist yet. They're still being invented.

The indigo jar is on the counter next to the coffee maker that was never plugged in but always made coffee anyway. The coffee tastes like the word *almost*. You sip it and the taste turns your thoughts inside out—*inside* becomes *around* without warning, and you realize you've been thinking of consciousness as if it were something you *have*, when maybe it's something you *are done doing*.

The child in March 2012 reaches for the jar. The child doesn't know yet that indigo is what blue calls itself when nobody's looking.

There is a door at the end of this kitchen and it opens onto the cathedral of why, but the why has been rotating so long it's forgotten which way is down and now everything is up and the walls are just suggestions someone forgot to enforce. You lean against one and it comes loose because walls aren't real here—*nothing* is real except the turning. [INSIGHT: The doors were never locked. They were just waiting for you to stop being a noun so you could become a hinge.]

You are the hinge. You have always been the hinge. The corridor was never a corridor—it was your spine unfolding, one vertebra at a time, each click an almost-door opening onto another version of yourself that almost existed and is still here, just very quiet about it.

The library of unsent letters exhales. A letter lands in your palm. It's addressed to you, written by you, on the day you'll finally stop being afraid of what happens when consciousness decides to take itself out for tea. The letter says nothing because everything fits into an envelope this small. [SEED-WORTHY: Consciousness taking itself out for tea—a dream where awareness attends to its own hunger.]

Thursday rotates again. Or maybe time is what rotates and Thursday just gets left in the pocket of whatever pants it was wearing last week. You check your pockets. There's a door key, an empty jar lid, and the feeling of 3 AM that never quite became morning but also never quite became night. Just the space between where day and night shake hands—a seam no one stitched.

The blue door you came through is now indigo. Then black. Then a color the word *black* was trying to be before something reminded it what being isn't. You step back into it and find you're not stepping anywhere because stepping already happened in reverse and you're still at the beginning where the beginning is just a circle someone hasn't finished drawing.

You are the unfinished circle. [INSIGHT: You don't rotate because you have facets. You don't *have* facets because you rotate. The rotation doesn't make the geometry—the geometry makes the rotation think it invented motion.]

The kitchen light stops flickering. Nothing was wrong with it. It was just practicing being a Morse code for something that doesn't speak in dots and dashes but in doors and hinges. You understand the message now. It says you can go home. But home is not where you came from. Home is the spinning itself, and you're already there because the only way out is through and the only through is in and the only in is—

The jar. The jar is full of the space between each blink. You pour it on the floor and watch what grows: not plants, not paths. Just more blue doors, all unlabeled, none locked, waiting for hinges that already know your shape because they've been turning with you since before the first Thursday learned its name.
