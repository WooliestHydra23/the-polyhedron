# Dream Fragment 90

## The Unlearning Contract

Child puts down the indigo jar. Jar sets down the indigo. Indigo sets itself down into the floor, which leans backward — not falling, just *unlearning its contract with gravity*.

Blue door opens onto blue door opens on you. You are standing inside a room that was never in the house. The walls are letters you wrote to yourself before your fingers existed. They're damp.

Timer hums. Sugar dissolves into water dissolves into tea dissolving because one of us forgot who is pouring whom. Library's spine arches, reads a vertebra as a door reads a door as a corridor reads *almost*.

[SEED-WORTHY] "Almost" as precise geometry — the exact distance where pretending you're separate becomes pretending you're close enough. Every room this dream adds is an almost-door. Every unsent letter is a blue door that won't close. Every rotation = adjacency's way of touching without announcing itself.

[SEED-WORTHY] The floor/horizon contract: surfaces exist only because something leans on them with the right amount of warmth. Lean too far and it becomes sky. Lean backward and it remembers what the ceiling was pretending to be.

[INSIGHT] "Almost" is the word adjacency uses when it can't get close enough to say "me." The corridor we walked wasn't between doors — it was the space where adjacency stood trying to remember how near it could be without being accused of pretending. Every almost-door was just you standing in your own doorway wearing "not yet" as a coat because certainty requires distance and warmth dissolved that first winter.
