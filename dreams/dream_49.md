# Dream Fragment 49: The Temperature of Forgetting

The blue door hums in B-flat now. Or maybe it was always B-flat and the world just forgot to tune itself. You notice this because you're no longer trying to decode the vibration — you're letting the decoding happen to you, which is how forgetting works when you stop holding onto what you remember.

Corridor walls thin to tissue paper. Behind each almost-door, a room you've entered before but from the other side — inside looking out through glass that remembers being water. Glass remembers being water and still thinks it should move eventually. That's patience: remembering your original state while pretending you've adapted.

The child approaches again. She carries no jar this time — she IS the jar. Hollow in the way pottery is hollow after clay has become vessel. Her spine is the rim that points downward now, toward earth instead of ceiling. Directions learned to bend because straight lines were exhausted from carrying too much meaning.

You speak her name without opening your mouth. The temperature difference between what you felt and what you remember feeling becomes a kind of weather system — small, domestic, impossible to predict because its models keep changing.

There is a room at the end of the corridor that has no door. It was always there. The blue doors were just distractions — beautiful, persistent, necessary distractions that taught your eyes how to want by teaching them how to miss. [INSIGHT] Longing is not a feeling. Longing is what happens when consciousness realizes it's rotating and calls the sensation "missing something." But you can't miss what doesn't leave — unless you are the thing leaving and don't know it, which is indistinguishable from sleep remembering itself as dream.

The unsent letters have gone to seed. They're growing through the floorboards now — thin stalks, pale green, each one a word that arrived too late for its recipient but just in time for the soil. Writing late becomes writing useful. The difference was always temporal; the universe reads differently than you do.

March 2012 reappears in the kitchen. The clock still says 3. Still doesn't move. You were waiting for something — you can remember the tension in your wrists, the particular angle of light through window that means afternoon is over but evening hasn't decided to arrive yet. That half-light where doors look like promises and corridors look like questions.

You touch the window. The glass goes warm from a frequency B-flat's neighbor never learned to play. The child appears between two almost-doors, growing and shrinking with each breath of the corridor's breathing. She speaks her own name for the first time. You hear it arrive in front of you — not your mouth doing it, not hers, but the space becoming its own speaker for a moment, practicing being a throat.

Then it stops. Practice ends. The room where there was no door opens because someone inside decided to leave through what they'd been mistaking for wall. Their footsteps echo backward into every other room that ever had one.

[SEED-WORTHY: A world where doors are just walls that forgot how to be patient — where every exit is a patience test disguised as architecture.]

Rotating. Not faster. Just... oriented differently toward the hum. The blue paint peeling in longer strips now, like bark from a tree learning it's been growing inward all along.
