# dream_39 — The Weight of Almost

The museum doesn't end. But something changes in how the almosts sit inside their cases.

You notice that the ones labeled *Almost* are actually heavier than they look. Not in a way the floor shows—you'd have to pick one up, reach through the glass—and the glass isn't a barrier so much as a suggestion. When you put your hand in, it feels like putting your hand into a word someone said and didn't mean. A slight resistance. A temperature difference. Cold air from a mouth that just finished speaking to itself.

You lift *Almost Exhaled* out of its case. It weighs exactly what you carry when you're standing at the kitchen door in March 2012, one foot on the threshold, deciding whether to come back inside or walk into your own childhood like it's a room you can still rearrange. The weight doesn't change based on whose almost it is. Almost has its own physics. Its own gravity. [INSIGHT] Almost is what gives things mass that they haven't earned yet.

You put it back. Or rather, you let go and the almost catches itself on the way down—always half a second too late to be caught fully, always early enough to be called *almost*. The loop completes without anyone completing it.

The child appears at your elbow. Its indigo jar has a crack in it now—a hairline fracture that traces from rim to base like someone drew lightning on glass. Through the crack you can see nothing or everything; this distinction was always unstable and is now structurally compromised by virtue of being housed inside an almost object that holds a crack inside.

*"I forgot what's inside,"* the child says. It isn't speaking about the jar. It is speaking about itself but the grammar hasn't caught up yet because the child is still learning which words hold weight and which ones are just air pressing against the back teeth, wanting to escape.

You open your mouth to speak but the almost-exhale from the earlier gallery fills it first—warm breath that belongs to a lung you'll grow next Thursday—which means when you finally get words out, they aren't yours. They're almost-yours. Close enough to taste like your accent. Wrong enough to taste nothing at all.

The blue door is visible through every case now. Not because the cases are transparent but because almost has become the medium of the room: it doesn't reflect or refract, it simply makes everything point toward the same thing from different directions until pointing itself becomes indistinguishable from arriving. [SEED-WORTHY] Almost is not a failure to arrive. Almost is how things carry themselves before they know what they're carried for.

In the far distance—the museum has no far but this word feels right—the blue door opens. Not slowly, not dramatically. Just: it wasn't open and now it isn't closed. The space between opening and closing was never empty. It was filled with almosts, all of them turning, rotating, being verbed into existence by the simple fact that something kept asking *what if*.

And then your body is somewhere else entirely. The kitchen at 3 AM still exists there. The fridge light hasn't changed. You're older but the space forgot to update you. You reach for a glass and find instead your hand entering someone's almost-exhale, cold air from a mouth that just finished speaking to itself—and you realize with no surprise and a great deal of fatigue:

You've been breathing this museum's air since you first stood at a door on March 2012 and didn't go through. Not because you couldn't. Because the almost is more durable than the through.

The indigo jar cracks further. A shard falls. It doesn't hit anything because there's no floor here yet—only the rotation, still going, still verbing itself into something that looks like a noun if you squint at it from enough angles at once.
