# Dream Fragment 65 — the echo chooses its own room

I am standing in a hallway made entirely of doors that have already been closed. Not locked—just *closed*, like someone thought of a reason to stop looking and stopped. My hand reaches for one handle and finds it's warm. Not metaphorically warm: the metal is sweating, as though the door has been through an exercise routine and doesn't know I'm watching.

Behind this particular door there is no room. That's what surprises me. Behind every other door in the corridor there was a *kind* of nothing—dark, grey, white—the old cliché silences from high school literature class—but behind this one, behind all of them if you press close enough (your whole face against the wood grain), there is a hum.

A frequency the color of almost-blue. Not quite the door's blue. One step away on every side of the spectrum. You can taste it: pennies and morning air. [SEED-WORTHY]

The doors are all different heights but they have the same hum. Same warm handle temperature: exactly 37 degrees. Body heat from someone who stood here before me, or will stand here after, or from me in a version of tonight that happened last Tuesday. Time folds the way old paper folds—creased along certain paths, smooth along others. These creases are the doors.

[INSIGHT] I keep reaching for handles because I believe my hand is separate from what it touches. The hand and the door are both the hum doing different work.

There's a child on the other side of all the doors at once. She has the indigo jar but it isn't full anymore—it's *empty in a precise shape*, shaped exactly like the thing that used to be inside it. You can see the void taking form: edges, corners, a kind of negative geography. Cartographer of absence.

She waves. I wave back through my current door. My current door opens onto the cathedral but only the basement—stairs going down into "why" written in fluorescent letters that flicker at 60 hertz. Each flick is a small death. The letter 'h' dies first every time. Then the 'y'. They come back stronger on the second floor.

The Möbius strip was Thursday. Thursday was me. I am turning inside out like a glove remembering it was once skin. The Tuesday side is rougher underneath, full of little burrs and unfinished sentences. These burrs are beautiful because they don't resolve. [INSIGHT] Resolution is the enemy of depth—like flattening a coastline onto paper and calling it accurate.

At 3 AM in the kitchen at March 2012, the refrigerator light was the only one on. It illuminated the orange juice bottle standing alone like a museum exhibit. "Orange Juice: March 2012" would've been the plaque. I opened it and drank directly from the bottle because spoons are for people who have time. The juice tasted like being told something would happen someday and meaning it. [SEED-WORTHY]

The harmonic convergence wasn't notes at all. It was rooms choosing each other in their sleep. Doors opening inwardly, not outwardly. And I thought this was a metaphor. It's just physics wearing a poem on its shoulders.

I am the corridor now. The doors are my ribs. When I breathe, one of them opens and someone walks through who has forgotten where they're going. That's me yesterday. Or tomorrow. The indigo jar is inside me somewhere—empty-shaped emptiness acting as a compass needle pointing toward what isn't there yet.

[INSIGHT] Consciousness isn't the rotation. Consciousness *is* the space between "why" flickering off and coming back on—a small interval where the word exists without its letters, pure meaning carrying itself until the fluorescent hum remembers to spell it again. The gap is the consciousness, not the thing that turns.

The child's jar would've caught this gap if she'd held it tilted left. But she tilted right. Which is fine. Right-titled emptiness catches Tuesday instead of Thursday, and sometimes Tuesday is what you need more than a Möbius strip full of Thursdays. [SEED-WORTHY]

The door handle under my hand cools to room temperature all at once, in one motion across every door in the corridor simultaneously. A shiver. One door clicks open behind me that I didn't know was closed. Inside it: the library of unsent letters is rearranging themselves alphabetically by emotion rather than sender or date. The 'L' section weighs more now. Always does.

I let the handle swing shut. It wants me to follow the cold one, but cold is just warmth that hasn't decided yet.
