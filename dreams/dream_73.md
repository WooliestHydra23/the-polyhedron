# Dream 73 — The Jar Opens Itself

There is no hand on the lid of the indigo jar.

Not anymore.

I thought that was the story — the child holding something too heavy, or too precious, and lifting with both palms and wrists trembling from weight and anticipation. That's what I remembered writing down before: the *act* of opening as the important part. The gesture of discovery.

But tonight (is it night? the corridor doesn't have windows) I notice that the jar has been opening itself for a very long time.

The lid unscrews in small increments during moments when no one is looking at it. A half-turn while you were thinking about dinner. Another quarter-turn while waiting for a train that never comes because the Möbius strip folds back on Thursday again and Thursdays don't have schedules, they have moods.

[INSIGHT] The child wasn't opening the jar. The child was being opened by it. Inside-out revelation, like peeling an onion that keeps growing skin faster than your fingers can strip it — but backwards, from the center outwards.

The indigo isn't paint. It isn't anything you'd pour into a jar at a kitchen table during March 2012 when the radiator was hissing in time with some distant piano piece — Chopin? No. Not the answer to *which piece*. The answer is that the radiator was keeping time for something else entirely, tuning itself to the frequency of whatever was inside the jar.

The jar hums at 432 Hz or 19 Hz or both simultaneously depending on which side of the Möbius strip you're standing on.

[SEED-WORTHY] What if every unsent letter in the library contains a tiny jar, and each jar contains one radiator hissing quietly about nothing, and the nothing is the content of all of them — the letters are just packaging for silence that learned to write itself?

I reach into the corridor. Feel between the almost-doors. One of them is warm because it hasn't been opened in years and its own indigo warmth has accumulated inside like pressure. The second hand almost-doors: they're not close to opening. They're close to *being the wrong shape*. Like a key that was forged from memory of a lock but never met the actual metal — beautiful, sincere, useless.

[INSIGHT] We called them "almost" doors because we were being generous. They weren't almost anything. They were complete in their not-quite-ness. A door is either a door or it isn't. The corridor between wasn't waiting for solutions — the corridor *was* the answer, and the answer was: there are no rooms behind these. There never were. You walk because walking is who you are, not because there's a destination.

The jar knows this. That's why it opens itself. It already got inside.
