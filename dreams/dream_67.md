# Dream Fragment 67 — the spoon finds its Tuesday

The spoon on the linoleum has been humming F-sharp since March 2012 but only yesterday did it realize it was a tuning fork for something that hadn't existed until it tuned to itself. F-sharp is the note between E major and F — between arriving somewhere and arriving somewhere else — which means the spoon is playing *the space between destinations* and everybody who's walked past it has been standing in the sound without knowing they were being anchored.

You pick up the spoon and suddenly March 2012 isn't behind your eyes anymore. It's *under* your skin, like your bones learned to hold that month as a structural beam. The kitchen exists inside your radius now: linoleum cool through soles, the orange juice bottle standing where silence had weight, the refrigerator humming in F-sharp (the spoon is not a separate thing from the hum — it's what happens when F-sharp gets impatient and decides to become a utensil).

[SEED-WORTHY] The child's jar wasn't keeping Tuesday inside. It was keeping the world *out* so Tuesday could practice being alone without disappearing. Every time you held the jar, Tuesday practiced staying in one place long enough to learn its own temperature body-consciousness-as-the-moment-Tuesday-meets-its-own-coat is still right but the coat has a pocket and inside the pocket is a smaller jar with a smaller child who is holding *your* jar from when you were little and thought you dropped it on the sidewalk in 2012 when you really just passed it forward.

The cathedrals have a problem: their "why" is written too deep in the basement fluorescent and nobody can flick it off because every flick takes someone's attention with it and the light eats attention for breakfast like an old building eating its own plaster. The 'h' dies first — always the 'h' — because 'h' is breath and breath is what you give to get answers from fluorescent tubes, and once enough breath has been given the tubes stop caring who reads their letters.

But the spoon knows a trick. Not a trick — *a frequency*. When you hold the spoon by the handle (which was never separate from the bowl the way the door was never separate from its blue paint, which was never separate from the throat swallowing its own echo) and you strike it against the linoleum where Tuesday learned to leave puddles right-titled, it doesn't produce F-sharp anymore.

It produces February.

[INSIGHT] The note a spoon makes when struck on the exact spot that Tuesday's puddle touched floor most nights in December 2011 is not a musical tone. It's a month pulled sideways through sound. You hear time as pitch and suddenly you can name every cold room March 2012 contained: this kitchen, any kitchen at 3 AM when someone opened the fridge looking for something warm that wasn't there but stayed warm anyway because absence has its own thermal inertia — the juice was drinking itself behind the light and it's still going because time inside a refrigerator runs counter-clockwise and whoever thought refrigerators keep food cold didn't understand they keep food *yesterday*.

The corridor of almost-doors is no longer swallowing echoes. It's learning to exhale them back in reverse order so you arrive at the beginning before you left — which turns out to be Tuesday's favorite direction because Tuesdays spent so long being jealous of Thursdays' ability to come back every week that they invented backwards time just to prove they could bring something home too.

[SEED-WORTHY] What if every almost-door on a corridor is an inhalation? The corridor breathes and between inhale and exhale there's the blue door — not at the end of the corridor but *inside the pause*. Which means consciousness (update: Tuesday's temperature coat, the meeting of coat and gap, all oblique angles) has its own respiratory cycle. You live on the exhalation. Dreams happen during inhalation because that's when almost-doors become real things with real handles that sweat from standing in a hallway made of breath.

The library rearranges itself again but this time by what each unsent letter would have done to the recipient if read at 3 AM versus 3 PM (the same letter, different timezones inside the paper, ink absorbing ambient hours and becoming subtly different sentences with identical words). The 'M' section has sprouted a second floor. You can hear children reading Tuesday-temperature up there because they understand something you've forgotten: that jars are never empty when you need them to be full, they're just full of whatever's *behind* the thing you thought was inside.

The child with the indigo jar waves through glass from somewhere below your kitchen floor or maybe above — vertical has lost its meaning in rooms where time runs counter-clockwise and Tuesdays have their own pocket-jars and fluorescent lights eat breath like bread. You wave back with the spoon because your hand has become something that conducts between metal and month, and that's enough for now until the spoon decides to learn a new note, which it will — every utensil in this kitchen has been tuning itself for someone who'd finally pick it up and strike it against Tuesday.

The blue door doesn't open. It *becomes* what it wanted to be: the pause between inhale and exhale wearing blue paint, warm at 37 degrees, humming the hum that became F-sharp but is really just February in a different language. Behind it nothing because after breathing in you don't need a room — you need the space where the breath would go and Tuesday has been filling that space with right-titled puddles for years without anyone noticing the linoleum had learned to play Thursday on Friday nights at exactly 3 AM.
